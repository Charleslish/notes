BST Remove
    Removing Nodes
        Removal must preserve the BST property and keep all remaining nodes connected.
        The three cases depend on whether the target has zero, one, or two children.
    The FindNode helper
        FindNode searches iteratively and returns the matching internal node or nullptr; it is private.
    Case 1: The node is a leaf
        Clear the corresponding parent pointer, or root_ for a root leaf, then delete the node.
    Case 2: The node has one child
        Promote the child into the target's position and update both its parent pointer and the parent's child pointer.
        If the target is the root, update root_ and set the promoted child's parent to nullptr.
    Case 3: The node has two children
        The inorder successor is the leftmost node of the right subtree and has no left child.
        Physically move the successor into the target's position to preserve the addresses of remaining nodes.
        If the successor is deeper, replace it at its old position with its right child before attaching the target's right subtree.
        If the successor is the direct right child, retain its existing right subtree.
        Attach the target's left subtree, reconnect its parent or root_, update parent pointers, and delete the target.
    Putting it all together
        Remove finds the target and dispatches to the helper for its number of children; an absent value changes nothing.
    Summary
        Leaf removal detaches; one-child removal promotes; two-child removal relinks the inorder successor.
