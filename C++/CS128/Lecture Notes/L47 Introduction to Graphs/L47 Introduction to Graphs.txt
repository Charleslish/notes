Introduction to Graphs
    Motivation
        Graphs model objects whose relationships can include many connections, cycles, and multiple paths.
    What Is a Graph?
        A graph consists of vertices representing objects and edges representing relationships.
        Simple graphs have no self-loops or repeated edges in the same direction.
    Paths, Cycles, and Adjacency
        Adjacent vertices share an edge; a path follows edges without repeating vertices.
        A cycle returns to its starting vertex without repeating other vertices or edges.
    Connected Components
        A connected component is a maximal group of mutually reachable vertices in an undirected graph.
        An isolated vertex forms its own component.
    Unweighted and Weighted Graphs
        Edges can represent connections alone or carry numerical weights.
    Unweighted Graphs
        Path length is the number of edges traversed.
    Weighted Graphs
        A path's length usually means the sum of its edge weights.
    Undirected and Directed Graphs
        Edge direction determines which paths are available.
    Undirected Graphs
        An undirected edge can be followed in either direction.
    Directed Graphs
        Directed paths must follow the arrows; a reverse edge is a separate relationship.
    Relating Graphs to Familiar Structures
        Linked lists and trees are graphs with additional structural constraints.
    Linked Lists
        A singly linked list forms a directed path; bidirectional links can be modeled as undirected edges.
    Trees
        A tree is a connected undirected graph without cycles and has exactly one path between any two vertices.
        A tree with V vertices has V - 1 edges.
    Adding and Removing Vertices and Edges
        Graph operations modify both size and connectivity.
    Adding a Vertex
        A new isolated vertex begins as a separate component.
    Adding an Edge
        An edge creates a relationship between existing vertices.
    Removing an Edge
        Removing an edge preserves its endpoints.
    Removing a Vertex
        Removing a vertex also removes all incident edges.
    What These Operations Change
        Joining different components merges them; adding a new edge within a component creates a cycle.
        Removing a bridge splits a component; removing a cycle edge leaves its component connected.
    Getting a Vertex's Neighbors
        Neighbors are one edge away; directed graphs distinguish out-neighbors from in-neighbors.
        GetNeighbors returns out-neighbors when direction is unspecified.
    Degree
        Degree counts neighbors; directed graphs distinguish out-degree and in-degree.
        In an undirected graph, the sum of all degrees equals 2E.
    Summary
        Vertices, edges, paths, cycles, components, direction, weights, and degree describe graph structure.
